How to Use

  1. Drop your photo into the area above. On first use the on-device engine (~13 MB) downloads once, then it is cached by your browser.
  2. Brush over the object you want removed — cover it fully, shadows included. The red overlay shows what will be erased.
  3. Press Erase Selected. The area is filled in from its surroundings. Brush and erase again for the next object; Undo Last reverts one pass.
  4. Press Download Result — the edited copy downloads and your original file stays untouched.

On-device inpainting — private, and honest about limits

Object removers usually upload your photo to a server where an AI model fills the gap. This tool runs the classical TELEA inpainting algorithm (OpenCV compiled to WebAssembly) inside your browser: the brushed region is reconstructed by propagating surrounding pixels inward, and nothing is ever transmitted.

Honest expectations: on even or softly textured backgrounds (sky, walls, grass, skin, paper) results are clean. Large objects on intricate backgrounds may leave smudges a server-side AI would avoid — brush the remnant and apply a second light pass. Only the brushed area is recomputed; every other pixel stays original.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are my photos uploaded to a server?

No. The tool downloads a computer-vision engine (OpenCV compiled to WebAssembly, about 13 MB, cached after the first use) and runs the erase-and-fill entirely in your browser. Your photo never leaves your device — which is exactly why it is safe for family photos and sensitive documents.

How does the filling work, and how good is it?

It uses the classical TELEA inpainting algorithm: the brushed area is filled by smoothly propagating the surrounding pixels inward. It works very well for small and medium objects on relatively even backgrounds — power lines, text stamps, skin blemishes, passers-by on grass or sky. For large objects on complex backgrounds a server-side AI inpainter will look better; the trade-off here is complete privacy.

Can I erase several things in one photo?

Yes. Brush and apply as many rounds as you like — each Erase pass works on the current result, so you can remove one object, then the next. Undo reverts the last pass, and Reset returns to the original photo at any time.

Does the rest of the photo lose quality?

No. Only the brushed region is recomputed; every pixel outside your brush strokes stays exactly as it was. Very large photos are processed at a slightly reduced working resolution for speed, but only inside the erased area — the untouched parts keep their original pixels on export.

What should I brush exactly?

Cover the whole object plus a small margin around it — shadows and outlines included. If a faint ghost remains, brush over the remnant and apply again; two light passes usually beat one heavy pass.
