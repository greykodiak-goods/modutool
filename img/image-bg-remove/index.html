How to Use

  1. Drop your image into the area above. On first use the on-device engine (~13 MB) downloads once, then it is cached.
  2. Drag a box around the subject. Make the box slightly generous — everything fully outside is treated as background. The cutout runs automatically.
  3. Check the checkerboard preview. Switch to the keep brush to restore parts that were wrongly removed, or the erase brush to clean up leftover background.
  4. Pick edge softness and a new background (transparent PNG, white, or a color), then click Download Result.

Private by design — and honest about the trade-off

Most background removers upload your photo to a server, run an AI model there, and send the result back. This tool ships the vision engine to your browser instead (OpenCV compiled to WebAssembly) and runs the classical GrabCut algorithm on your device. Your photo never leaves your computer.

The honest trade-off: on complex scenes or fine hair, a server-side neural network will beat GrabCut. On product shots, logos, documents, and clearly separated subjects, results are clean — and the keep/erase brushes close most of the gap. If your image is sensitive (people, IDs, unreleased products), on-device is the safer choice.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is my photo really never uploaded anywhere?

Correct. This tool bundles a computer-vision engine (OpenCV compiled to WebAssembly) that runs inside your browser, so the whole separation happens on your device. The engine file itself is downloaded once from this site, but your photo never leaves your computer or phone.

How good are the results compared to AI background removers?

This tool uses the classical GrabCut algorithm rather than a neural network. On product photos, logos, and subjects that stand out from the background it produces clean results, especially after a little brushing. On busy backgrounds or wispy hair it is less precise than server-based AI tools — the trade-off is that your image stays completely private. Use the keep/erase brushes to fix edges.

Why do I need to drag a box first?

The box tells the algorithm roughly where your subject is: everything clearly outside is treated as definite background, and the algorithm then iteratively separates subject from background inside. A slightly generous box that fully contains the subject works best.

What formats can I download?

Transparent PNG is the default — ideal for putting the subject on any new design. You can also choose a white or custom solid color background, which is handy for marketplace product photos and ID-style pictures; solid-background copies can also be saved as JPG.

Why is the first load slow?

The on-device vision engine is about 13 MB and is fetched the first time you use the tool, then cached by your browser. That download is the price of never sending your photos to a server — after the first visit it starts much faster.
