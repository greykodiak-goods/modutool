How to Use

  1. Drop your video into the area above. On first use the on-device engine (~31 MB) downloads once and is cached — after that it starts instantly.
  2. Play the preview and set the Start and End sliders. The preview jumps to the slider position as you drag, so you can find the exact moment.
  3. Click Trim & Download. The kept part is copied without re-encoding — nearly instant, no quality loss — and downloads as filename_trim in the original format.

Why an in-browser trimmer beats upload sites

Video is where "no upload" matters most: uploading a 500 MB clip to a converter site takes minutes before any work starts, and you are trusting a stranger's server with your footage. This tool ships FFmpeg — the same engine professionals use — compiled to WebAssembly, so the cut happens on your device: processing starts instantly, private footage stays private, and there is no watermark and no artificial size cap.

Honest note: by default we cut with stream copy (no re-encoding). It is lossless and nearly instant, but cuts snap to keyframes, so the actual start can be up to a couple of seconds before your chosen point. That is the right trade-off for clipping recordings, lectures, and gameplay; frame-exact editing belongs in a video editor.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is my video really never uploaded?

Correct. The tool downloads FFmpeg compiled to WebAssembly (about 31 MB, once, then cached) and cuts your video inside the browser. Nothing is transmitted — which also means no upload wait: a 500 MB video starts processing instantly.

Why is trimming so fast, and why can the cut point shift slightly?

By default the tool copies the video stream without re-encoding (lossless and nearly instant). Video files can only be cut cleanly at keyframes, so the actual start may snap up to a couple of seconds earlier than your slider position. For most clips this is invisible; frame-exact cutting would require re-encoding the whole clip.

Does the output lose quality or get a watermark?

No and no. Stream copy means the kept part is bit-for-bit identical to the original — same resolution, same bitrate, no generation loss, and there is never a watermark.

What formats are supported?

MP4, WebM, MOV and MKV work well. The output keeps the same container and codecs as the input. Some exotic codecs may fail to open — if that happens, the tool tells you instead of producing a broken file.

Is there a file size limit?

We impose none, but processing happens in your browser's memory, so the practical limit depends on your device — roughly 1–2 GB on a desktop and less on phones. Very large files may fail to load; that is the honest trade-off of never using a server.
